## 2. Overview

**One-line essence:**

> The whole-leaf concentrated green tea — Japanese ceremonial
> tradition meets modern wellness staple, delivering the calm-
> focused alertness of high L-theanine combined with concentrated
> caffeine, all in vivid jade-green powdered form.

**Short description:**

> Matcha is finely powdered, shade-grown green tea leaves
> (*Camellia sinensis*), consumed whole rather than steeped.
> The plants are shaded for 20-30 days before harvest to suppress
> photosynthesis, which boosts L-theanine, chlorophyll, and
> caffeine content while reducing bitterness-producing catechins.
> After harvest, the leaves are steamed, dried, deveined, and
> stone-ground into ultra-fine powder (called *tencha* before
> grinding, *matcha* after). The defining preparation is whisking
> 1-2 grams of powder into hot water with a bamboo whisk
> (*chasen*) until frothy — there's no steeping or filtration;
> the entire leaf is consumed. This whole-leaf consumption gives
> matcha dramatically higher concentrations of all bioactive
> compounds compared to steeped green tea, including significantly
> more caffeine (~60-140mg per serving) and L-theanine (the
> amino acid responsible for the characteristic "calm focus"
> effect). Originated in Tang Dynasty China, brought to Japan
> by Buddhist monks in the 12th century, codified in the
> Japanese tea ceremony (*chanoyu*) by the 15th century, and
> exploded into global wellness popularity in the 2010s-2020s.

## 3. Sensory — flavor & aroma

**Primary flavor notes:**

- umami
- vegetal
- grassy
- sweet
- oceanic

**Aroma notes:**

> Distinctive vivid green character — the smell of fresh-cut
> grass meets seaweed meets sweet vegetable. Quality matcha
> smells brightly fragrant when fresh; stale or oxidized matcha
> smells dull and hay-like. The aroma is much more concentrated
> than gyokuro's because the whole leaf is present rather than
> just the water-soluble compounds.
>
> Variety-specific aromatics:
> - **Ceremonial grade (first harvest, *ichibancha*):** Sweetest,
>   most umami-forward, almost dessert-like aromatic depth
> - **Premium grade:** More vegetal, slightly more catechin-bitter
> - **Culinary grade (later harvests):** More astringent, more
>   bitter; better for cooking/lattes than straight whisking

**Mouthfeel:**

> Genuinely thick and creamy — the suspended powder gives matcha
> a body that no steeped tea matches. The traditional whisk
> creates a fine foam (*koimatsu*) on top that's part of the
> sensory experience. Quality matcha is smooth and almost
> velvety; lower grades feel gritty or chalky. The umami
> register persists in the mouth long after swallowing.

**Basic tastes:**

> - `umami` (4-5) — defining quality; the L-theanine register
> - `sweet` (2-3) — natural sweetness in ceremonial grade
> - `bitter` (1-3) — depends on grade and preparation; quality
>   matcha is barely bitter, culinary grade more so
> - `astringent` (1-2) — subtle in ceremonial, more pronounced
>   in lower grades

> **Sensory note:** Matcha represents the most umami-forward
> ingredient in the catalog. Side-by-side with gyokuro (also
> shade-grown), matcha's whole-leaf nature gives a more intense,
> thicker umami than gyokuro's cleaner extracted form.

## 4. Brewing — baseline parameters

| Parameter | Value | Source | Notes |
|-----------|-------|--------|-------|
| **temp range (°C)** | [70, 80] | traditional Japanese | **Critical: never use boiling water.** High temperature destroys L-theanine and over-extracts catechins, producing harsh bitter cup |
| **time range (seconds)** | [15, 30] | traditional Japanese | **Whisking, not steeping.** Time is whisk duration, not infusion time. The mechanic is fundamentally different from steeped tea |
| **caffeine (mg per ~8oz cup)** | 60-140 | ref-richingmatcha-2025 | **Highest caffeine in catalog when prepared traditionally.** Ceremonial grade ~120-140mg per 2g serving; standard prep ~60-70mg per 1g serving |
| **dose** | 1-2 grams (1/2 - 1 tsp) of powder per 60-80ml water | traditional Japanese | Much smaller water volume than steeped tea — concentrated preparation |

> **The whole-leaf consumption mechanic:**
>
> Matcha doesn't fit the catalog's standard brewing model
> (temperature + time = extraction). Instead:
>
> 1. Sift powder into a bowl (*chawan*) to break up clumps
> 2. Add water at 70-80°C (around 175°F) — usually 60-80ml for
>    standard *usucha* preparation
> 3. Whisk vigorously with bamboo whisk (*chasen*) in a W or M
>    motion for 15-30 seconds until frothy
> 4. Drink the entire bowl — there's nothing to filter or
>    discard
>
> Because the entire leaf is consumed, matcha delivers:
> - All water-soluble compounds (caffeine, L-theanine, simpler
>   catechins) — same as steeped tea
> - All water-insoluble compounds (chlorophyll, fiber, larger
>   catechin polymers, fat-soluble vitamins) — unique to
>   powder consumption
>
> This is why effects feel different from gyokuro despite the
> same starting plant material. The vocabulary captures this by
> rating matcha higher on `focus` and `energy` than gyokuro.

> **Grade matters more than for any other catalog ingredient:**
>
> | Grade | Use | Caffeine | L-theanine |
> |-------|-----|----------|-----------|
> | Ceremonial (*Ichibancha*) | Drink straight; tea ceremony | ~120-140 mg/2g | Highest |
> | Premium / Barista | Daily drinking; lattes | ~80-120 mg/2g | High |
> | Culinary | Baking, cooking, smoothies | ~40-80 mg/2g | Moderate |
>
> Drinking culinary-grade straight is unpleasant for most users
> — too bitter and astringent. Cooking with ceremonial-grade is
> wasteful (cost-prohibitive, and the delicate flavors are lost
> in baked goods). User-facing copy should surface grade
> distinction.

> - Usucha works with ceremonial OR premium grade

> ceremonial grade in lattes and trying to drink culinary grade

## 7. Safety & heads-up

**Caffeine considerations:**

> **Matcha contains the most caffeine of any catalog ingredient
> when prepared traditionally.** Ceremonial grade can deliver
> 120-140mg per 2g serving — comparable to a strong shot of
> espresso. Considerations:
>
> - **Caffeine sensitivity:** Users sensitive to caffeine should
>   start with smaller doses (0.5-1g) or culinary grade
> - **Pregnancy:** Standard caffeine guidance applies (under
>   200mg/day). One ceremonial-grade matcha consumes most of
>   that allowance
> - **Anxiety disorders:** L-theanine moderates jitter risk vs.
>   coffee, but caffeine is still caffeine — high-dose matcha
>   can still trigger anxiety in sensitive individuals
> - **Sleep:** Despite L-theanine's calming effect, matcha at
>   typical doses past midday can affect sleep onset

**Lead content concerns:**

> Tea plants accumulate lead from soil, and whole-leaf
> consumption means matcha drinkers consume any accumulated
> lead rather than discarding it with the leaves. Modern
> ceremonial-grade Japanese matcha is generally tested and low
> in lead; cheaper and less-regulated sources (especially
> Chinese culinary grade) have shown elevated levels in
> independent testing. Recommendation: source matcha from
> Japan with verified third-party testing if available.
> `attested`

**Known interactions:**

> - **Anticoagulants (especially warfarin):** Matcha contains
>   significant Vitamin K from the chlorophyll-rich whole-leaf
>   consumption. Can interfere with warfarin dosing. Users on
>   warfarin should maintain consistent matcha intake or
>   discuss with their physician. `established`
> - **Iron absorption:** Tannins in matcha can reduce non-heme
>   iron absorption when consumed with iron-rich meals.
>   Recommendation: separate matcha from iron-rich meals by
>   1-2 hours, especially for users with iron deficiency.
>   `attested`
> - **Stimulant medications:** Matcha's caffeine adds to
>   prescribed stimulants (ADHD medications). Awareness rather
>   than contraindication. `attested`

**NOT a concern but sometimes claimed:**

> - **"Matcha cures cancer"** — Strong pre-clinical EGCG
>   anticancer signals; clinical translation is mixed. Standard
>   pattern. `folk` to `attested` depending on framing
> - **"Matcha causes liver damage"** — Some cases linked to
>   high-dose green tea extract supplements (not matcha tea);
>   consuming whole-leaf matcha at typical doses doesn't
>   reproduce these effects. `folk`

## 8. History & cultural context

**Plant origin:**

> *Camellia sinensis* shade-grown for matcha is cultivated
> primarily in Japan, with major production regions including
> Uji (Kyoto Prefecture — historic center, premium quality),
> Nishio (Aichi Prefecture — second-largest production center),
> Shizuoka, and Kagoshima. China also produces some matcha
> (and is the historical origin), but Japanese matcha is
> considered the standard. Some Korean, Chinese, and even US
> farms now produce matcha-style products of varying quality.
> `verified`

**Historical timeline:**

> - **Tang Dynasty China (~7th-9th century):** Powdered green
>   tea ("frothy tea") originates as Chinese imperial drink.
>   Lu Yu's *Cha Jing* (*The Classic of Tea*, 760 CE)
>   documents preparation methods. `verified`
> - **Song Dynasty China (~10th-13th century):** Powdered tea
>   reaches peak refinement; whisked preparation becomes
>   formalized in Chinese imperial court. `verified`
> - **1191 CE:** Buddhist monk Eisai brings green tea seeds
>   and powdered tea preparation from China to Japan,
>   establishing the foundation for what becomes matcha
>   tradition. `verified`
> - **13th-14th century Japan:** Powdered green tea adopted
>   by Japanese Buddhist monks, who use it for meditation
>   alertness during long sitting sessions. `verified`
> - **Ming Dynasty China (~14th-17th century):** China
>   abandons powdered tea for steeped loose leaf — the
>   tradition of powdered green tea essentially dies out in
>   China. `verified`
> - **15th century Japan:** Tea master Murata Jukō
>   establishes principles of *wabi-cha* (rustic tea
>   aesthetic). Sen no Rikyū (1522-1591) codifies what
>   becomes the formal Japanese tea ceremony (*chanoyu* or
>   *sadō*). The four principles emerge: *wa* (harmony),
>   *kei* (respect), *sei* (purity), *jaku* (tranquility).
>   `verified`
> - **Edo period (1603-1868):** Tea ceremony tradition
>   refines and spreads; *tencha* (the unground precursor)
>   commercial production develops; multiple schools of tea
>   ceremony emerge (Urasenke, Omotesenke, Mushakōjisenke).
>   `verified`
> - **Meiji period onward:** Matcha cultivation industrializes
>   somewhat while tea ceremony tradition continues.
>   `verified`
> - **Late 20th century:** Matcha begins appearing in
>   Japanese-style desserts and ice cream, exporting flavor
>   profile globally before the powder itself.
> - **2010s-2020s:** Matcha explosion in Western wellness
>   markets. Drives modern consumer demand; transforms matcha
>   from ceremonial niche to mainstream wellness staple.
>   Lattes, smoothies, energy drinks, baked goods. Global
>   matcha market estimated at $3+ billion USD in 2025 and
>   growing rapidly. `verified`

**Cultural significance:**

> The Japanese tea ceremony (*chanoyu*) is one of the most
> formally codified beverage rituals in any culture. Its
> influence extends far beyond tea drinking:
>
> - **Architecture:** Tea rooms (*chashitsu*) are foundational
>   to Japanese architectural aesthetics; the *roji* (tea garden)
>   path, the small entrance forcing humility, the alcove
>   (*tokonoma*) for seasonal display
> - **Aesthetics:** The principles of *wabi-sabi* (beauty in
>   imperfection and impermanence) emerge directly from tea
>   ceremony culture
> - **Calligraphy and pottery:** Tea ceremony drove development
>   of distinctive Japanese pottery styles (Raku, Hagi, Bizen,
>   Karatsu) and scroll calligraphy
> - **Mindfulness practice:** The ceremony's slow precision
>   anticipates modern mindfulness movements; *ichigo ichie*
>   ("one time, one meeting" — each gathering is unrepeatable)
>   captures a depth of presence
> - **Modern globalization:** Matcha is one of Japan's most
>   successful cultural exports; the Japanese aesthetic of
>   precision, seasonality, and quiet attention has influenced
>   global design, food, and wellness
>
> User-facing copy can honor this depth without either
> overclaiming spiritual benefits or treating ceremony as
> cosmetic decoration. `verified`

**Modern clinical evidence:**

> Matcha has accumulated solid clinical research, primarily
> through extension of green tea / EGCG / L-theanine literature:
>
> - **Cognitive function:** L-theanine + caffeine combination
>   shows clinically significant cognitive enhancement (Sohail
>   2021 systematic review of 10 RCTs). Effects on sustained
>   attention, working memory, and reaction time
> - **L-theanine alone:** Multiple RCTs show L-theanine alone
>   reduces stress markers, improves sleep quality (despite
>   matcha's caffeine), and increases alpha brain wave activity
> - **Cardiovascular markers:** Green tea catechins consistently
>   show modest cardiovascular benefits; matcha delivers higher
>   doses than steeped green tea
> - **Glucose regulation:** Catechin-rich green teas modestly
>   improve glucose handling; matcha extension is plausible
>   though less directly studied
> - **Antioxidant:** EGCG from whole-leaf matcha is among the
>   most concentrated dietary sources of polyphenol
>   antioxidants
> - **Therapeutic potential review:** Kochman et al. 2020 and
>   Sokary et al. 2023 reviews summarize matcha's emerging
>   research base
>
> Compared to gyokuro (steeped) the same compounds at higher
> concentrations through whole-leaf consumption. The clinical
> evidence base is robust but still maturing for matcha
> specifically (vs. green tea generally). `established`
> (cognitive function, antioxidant); `attested` (cardiovascular,
> glucose).

**TCM framing:**

> Powdered tea (*moca cha*, 抹茶) was central to Chinese tea
> culture during Tang and Song dynasties. Classical TCM
> classification of green tea: cooling, slightly bitter, with
> affinity for Heart, Lung, Kidney, Stomach meridians. Used
> for clearing heat, dispelling phlegm, sharpening the mind.
> `verified`

**Japanese aesthetic framing:**

> Within tea ceremony tradition, matcha embodies several
> aesthetic principles:
>
> - ***Ichigo ichie*** (一期一会): "One time, one meeting" —
>   each tea gathering is irreproducible and complete in
>   itself
> - ***Wabi-sabi***: Beauty in imperfection, impermanence,
>   and incompleteness
> - ***Mu shin*** (無心): The "mind without mind" — full
>   presence without grasping
>
> These framings can inform user-facing copy authentically
> without forcing them. `verified`

## 11. Research flags & open questions

1. **Schema reconciliation needed (similar to turmeric, ashwagandha).**
   Adding matcha requires fitting whole-leaf consumption into a
   schema designed around steeped extraction. The brewing
   parameters concept needs adaptation — maybe a `preparation`
   field distinguishing "steep" / "whisk" / "decoction" /
   "chew."

2. **The grade distinction is more important than for any other
   catalog ingredient.** Most catalog ingredients have minor
   grade variations; matcha's grade distinction is fundamental
   to user experience. Recommend surfacing in UI (perhaps as
   notes or as variants).

3. **The whole-leaf consumption mechanic is unique in the
   catalog.** Establishes a pattern that could apply to future
   additions: turmeric powder consumed in golden milk (effective
   "whole-rhizome consumption"), powdered ginger in chai,
   matcha-style preparations of other green teas (rare).
   Worth documenting as a preparation pattern.

4. **Caffeine ceiling moves up.** Previously the catalog's
   caffeine ceiling was assam at ~80mg/cup. Ceremonial matcha
   can deliver 120-140mg, comfortably exceeding. Worth
   reflecting in user-facing copy that mentions caffeine
   levels.

5. **Lead testing is a real concern.** Whole-leaf consumption
   makes lead exposure higher than for steeped tea where most
   lead stays in the discarded leaves. Worth surfacing in copy
   as guidance to source from regulated suppliers.

6. **The cultural depth challenge.** Tea ceremony has 800+
   years of refined tradition; user-facing copy should engage
   honestly without either trivializing or overclaiming.

7. **Pairing recommendations.** Matcha is traditionally
   consumed alone (the ceremonial preparation precludes
   blending). Modern uses include lattes (with milk), smoothies,
   and baked goods. Blend-recommendation logic should largely
   leave matcha as standalone.

8. **The gyokuro relationship.** Both come from shaded *Camellia
   sinensis*; matcha is leaves ground into powder, gyokuro is
   leaves steeped. Per Principle #18, separate ingredients —
   but worth surfacing the relationship for users curious
   about Japanese green tea family.

9. **Matcha latte popularity creates expectation friction.**
   Most users' matcha experience is sweetened lattes; the plain
   *usucha* preparation can be surprising (more bitter, more
   intense). User-facing copy should set expectations.

10. **Decaffeinated matcha exists but is rare.** Mostly used
    for late-evening or pregnancy-conscious consumption.
    Future catalog consideration if user demand surfaces.

## Addendum — `uplifting` transcribed, `soothing` cannot be (2026-08-02)

An audit found two effects this document prescribes that no brew point
ever carried. Only one of them is transcribable, and the reason is
structural rather than an oversight.

**§6 here is not a temperature gradient.** It is three preparations —
usucha, koicha, matcha latte — and the section says so outright: "the
standard GENTLE/STANDARD/STRONG extraction model doesn't fit matcha
cleanly because there's no extraction in the standard sense." The
shipped profile, like every other profile in the app, is a
temperature/time curve. The two axes are not the same axis.

### `uplifting` 2 — transcribed

Prescribed at **all three** preparations, and rated 2 in §5 on general
grounds ("the bright vegetal aromatic profile and sustained energy
combine to produce mood-positive effect"). General across preparations
means it survives the translation, so it now ships at 2 across the
70–80°C samples.

Not added at 90°C or 95°C. Those points are above anything §6
describes — matcha is whisked at 70–80°C and the hotter rows model a
scalded cup — and the profile already drops `calm` and most of `focus`
there. Adding a mood-positive claim to the two rows that exist to show
the drink going wrong would contradict the profile's own logic.

### `soothing` — not transcribable

Prescribed for koicha (1) and the latte (2), and absent from usucha.
Those differ by **concentration and by the presence of milk**, not by
temperature — koicha is 4–5g kneaded into 30–50ml, the latte is
matcha plus steamed milk. The profile has no axis for either. Placing
`soothing` at some temperature would be inventing a structure the
research doesn't support, purely to make an audit line go away.

Recorded instead as a known structural limit.
