## 2. Overview

**One-line essence:**

> The sharpest, brightest citrus peel — clean lift without bergamot's florality or orange's warmth.

**Short description:**

> Lemon peel is the dried rind of *Citrus limon*, the most
> direct and uncomplicated of the citrus peels in the
> catalog. Its essential oil is dominated by limonene
> (~70%) and beta-pinene (~10%), with citral (the same
> compound found in lemongrass) giving it its
> characteristic sharpness. Where bergamot reads as
> citrus-with-flowers and orange peel as citrus-with-warmth,
> lemon peel reads as just-citrus — clean, bright, almost
> aggressive in fresh form, softer when dried. Used widely
> across European tea traditions: Russian "lemon tea"
> (*chai s limonom*), Italian *canarino* (lemon peel
> infused alone or with bay leaf), and as the dominant
> note in commercial "wellness" and "immune" blends. Pairs
> exceptionally well with ginger (the citral-gingerol
> combination is a digestive classic), with black teas
> (cuts through tannin), and with chamomile (lifts a
> sleepy blend into a daytime one). The pith carries
> bitter flavonoids similar to orange peel; over-pulled
> lemon peel goes harshly bitter rather than just losing
> aroma.

## 3. Sensory — flavor & aroma

**Primary flavor notes:**

- citrus
- bright
- bitter
- aromatic

**Aroma notes:**

> Sharp, clean, unmistakably lemon. Brighter and more
> direct than any other citrus peel in the catalog —
> bergamot has flowers riding it, orange has sweetness
> riding it, lemon is just citrus. Volatiles dissipate
> faster than orange or bergamot, which is why lemon-
> heavy blends are best brewed fresh.

**Mouthfeel:**

> Light. Citral's slight astringent edge gives the cup
> some grip; pith adds a bitter backbone if any quantity
> is included. Cleaner than orange peel.

### 6d. Time-axis behavior

> Like orange peel, lemon peel has a clear inversion past
> ~6 minutes: volatile oils evaporate while pith bitter
> flavonoids continue to extract. The inversion is
> sharper for lemon than for orange because citral is more
> volatile. Long lemon-peel steeps go bitter without
> getting more aromatic.

## 7. Safety & heads-up

**headsUp field:**

> Some psoralens in lemon peel are mildly photosensitizing
> on skin in concentrated topical use; oral consumption at
> tea levels is safe. Dental note: highly acidic citrus
> infusions can soften enamel over time — rinse with
> water if drinking citrus teas frequently.

**Dosage cautions:**

> A teaspoon per cup is plenty. More than that and pith
> bitterness dominates the aroma you actually want.

**Not a concern but sometimes claimed:**

> "Lemon detoxifies" — there's no detoxification mechanism
> at play. The cup tastes clean and bright; that's the
> whole effect.

## Addendum — prose claim audit (2026-08-23)

**Sicilian citrus and the origins of the mafia — sourced.** The facts
list has carried this claim unsourced. It is well supported, and by
economic history rather than folklore: Dimico, Isopi and Olsson,
"Origins of the Sicilian Mafia: The Market for Lemons", *Journal of
Economic History* (2017), find a strong town-level association between
mafia presence and citrus production using the 1881-86 Damiani
inquiry.

The mechanism is specific to the crop. Demand rose sharply once citrus
was recognised as preventing scurvy — Messina's lemon exports went
from 740 barrels in 1837 to 20,707 by 1850 — and lemon groves are
unusually extortable: high value, walled and irrigated, dependent on
fast transport to distant ports, and trivially easy to rob overnight
compared with olives or grapes. A weak new Italian state could not
enforce property rights in western Sicily, so protection was sold by
the people best placed to supply the threat. `verified`
