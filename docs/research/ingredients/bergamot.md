## 2. Overview

**One-line essence:**

> Calabrian citrus peel — the aromatic perfume that defines Earl Grey, sitting between citrus brightness and floral calm.

**Short description:**

> Bergamot peel is the dried rind (or expressed oil) of *Citrus bergamia*, a small bitter citrus grown almost entirely along Calabria's Ionian coast in southern Italy. The fruit itself is inedibly bitter; its value lies almost entirely in the rind's volatile oil, which carries an unusually high concentration of linalool and linalyl acetate — the same calming compounds that drive lavender's pharmacology. This explains why bergamot reads as a citrus with a floral, uplifting, slightly sedative finish rather than a sharp citrus like lemon. Best known as the defining flavor of Earl Grey tea (named for 1830s British PM Charles Grey), and as a primary base note in Italian and French perfumery (eau de cologne began as bergamot-led). Aromatherapy studies have measured small reductions in anxiety and cortisol with bergamot inhalation, consistent with the linalool mechanism. The "bergapten" furocoumarins in unrefined bergamot oil are mildly photosensitizing on skin in concentrated topical use; oral consumption at tea levels is safe, but most modern bergamot used in tea/perfume is the bergapten-free (FCF) form for skin safety.

## 3. Sensory — flavor & aroma

**Primary flavor notes:**

- bergamot (a flavor descriptor of its own — irreducible to "citrus")
- citrus
- floral
- heady
- aromatic

**Aroma notes:**

> The defining quality is *citrus on a floral base* — bright top
> notes of lemon-orange peel that resolve into a soft, almost
> lavender-adjacent middle. In a cup it perfumes the steam more
> than it tastes on the tongue. Distinctly different from
> orange or lemon peel, which read as straight citrus.

**Mouthfeel:**

> Light. The peel itself contributes very little body; what you
> get is volatile aromatics riding the surface of whatever
> base tea or herbal carrier the bergamot is paired with.

## 7. Safety & heads-up

**headsUp field:**

> Photosensitive on skin in concentrated topical use (bergapten
> furocoumarins). Oral consumption at tea levels is safe; most
> modern food-grade bergamot is bergapten-reduced (FCF).

**Dosage cautions:**

> Pinches, not spoonfuls. A small amount (¼ tsp) carries the cup;
> more becomes overwhelming.

**Not a concern but sometimes claimed:**

> "Bergamot lowers cholesterol" — overstated; the studies were on
> standardized bergamot juice extract (BPF) at concentrations far
> above what tea provides. Tea-level bergamot is flavor, not
> therapy.

## Addendum — `focus` removed (2026-08-02)

An audit found `focus` 2–3 shipped at every brew point with no §5 row
behind it. The literature on bergamot is genuinely good, and it is
about something else.

> *A Neural Circuit for Bergamot Essential Oil-Induced Anxiolytic
> Effects.* Advanced Science (2025) —
> https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202406766
>
> *Bergamot Essential Oil Inhalation Improves Positive Feelings in the
> Waiting Room of a Mental Health Treatment Center* — pilot study.

Limonene (~36%), linalyl acetate (~31%) and linalool (~11%) carry
anxiolytic and mood-elevating activity through GABAergic and
serotonergic routes. That is exactly what §5 already rates: `uplifting`
3, "linalyl acetate + citrus volatiles consistently produce mood-lift
in aromatherapy trials", and `calm` 2 via the linalool overlap with
lavender.

**No study measures bergamot improving attention in healthy people.**
The nearest results are cognitive improvement in mild cognitive
impairment — a clinical population, not an attention effect — and
qualitative self-report from a PTSD study. What bergamot is unusual
for is being calming *without* sedating, and an absence of drowsiness
is not focus. That distinction matters here more than most places,
because bergamot's whole catalogue identity is Earl Grey, where the
focus a drinker feels is the black tea's caffeine and L-theanine.

Removed. `uplifting` 3 and `calm` 2 carry bergamot's sourced picture,
and the app's own vocabulary already names bergamot under uplifting.
