## 2. Overview

**One-line essence:**

> Tart North American berry — sharp, bright, the cleanest fruit-tartness alternative to hibiscus.

**Short description:**

> Cranberry is one of three fruits native to North America
> that became commercial crops (the others being blueberry
> and Concord grape). Indigenous peoples — Algonquian,
> Wampanoag, Lenape — used cranberries as food, dye, and
> medicine long before European contact, often pounded
> with deer fat into *pemmican*. The berry's sharp tartness
> comes from quinic, malic, and citric acids; its color
> comes from anthocyanins (the same pigment family that
> makes hibiscus and elderberry red). In tea, dried
> cranberry contributes a clean, bright tartness that's
> distinct from hibiscus's sour-floral register — drier,
> less floral, more "clean fruit." Cranberry's most
> persistent medicinal claim is for urinary tract
> infections; the proanthocyanidins (PACs) in cranberry
> do appear to inhibit bacterial adhesion to bladder walls
> in vitro and in some clinical trials, though tea-level
> doses are far below the therapeutic threshold. Cranberry
> contains compounds that interact with warfarin
> (anticoagulant) — anyone on blood thinners should
> consult a clinician before consuming concentrated
> cranberry products. Tea-level use is generally fine but
> worth flagging.

## 3. Sensory — flavor & aroma

**Primary flavor notes:**

- tart
- fruity
- cranberry
- bright

**Aroma notes:**

> Sharp, dry-bright fruit aroma — distinct from hibiscus
> (which is more floral-sour) and from rose hips (which is
> more honey-tart). Reads as "cranberry" in its own right —
> the flavor is irreducible to "tart fruit."

**Mouthfeel:**

> Light to medium, with a slight pucker. The tartness
> contributes a brightness that lifts heavy or sweet
> blends. Without something sweet to balance it, cranberry
> can feel astringent.

### 6d. Time-axis behavior

> Acids and pigments extract together; longer steeps give
> more tartness and deeper color. There's no bitter ceiling
> the way there is with citrus pith — cranberry just gets
> more intensely cranberry. The pairing partner (hibiscus,
> rose, apple) usually hits its ceiling first.

## 7. Safety & heads-up

**headsUp field:**

> Cranberry can interact with warfarin (anticoagulant)
> at concentrated doses — consult a clinician if you're on
> blood thinners. Tea-level use is generally fine but worth
> flagging. People with a history of kidney stones (oxalate
> type) should drink in moderation.

**Dosage cautions:**

> A teaspoon per cup balances well; more becomes
> aggressively tart without a sweet partner.

**Not a concern but sometimes claimed:**

> "Cranberry tea cures UTIs" — overstated. Cranberry's
> proanthocyanidins do reduce bacterial adhesion in lab
> studies, but tea-level doses are far below clinical
> thresholds. Not a treatment.
